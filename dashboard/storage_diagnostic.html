Category Growth Diagnostic · Storage & Organisers · Noon AE

The category is growing on traffic —
and quietly losing it on conversion.

GMV is up +47.2% YoY (Jul'26 vs Jul'25, clean full-month comparison) on a genuinely broad base — traffic, conversion and pricing are all up year-on-year. But the most recent month breaks that pattern: in the last 30 days, three of the category's biggest subcategories — Storage Box, Racks, Storage Basket — suffered a sudden, sharp conversion crash with no precedent anywhere in the prior 19 months of data, and it's still live as of the latest data.

Leak point: GV→ATC (add-to-cart rate) fell 3.3pp (18.4%→15.1%) even as GV surged — new traffic is browsing but adding to cart at a lower rate, and what does get added converts to orders slightly worse too (CVR −0.5pp). The funnel is wider at the top and leakier in the middle simultaneously.

No subcategory shows E (healthy funnel, falling ASP) at meaningful scale — the closest, Storage Set, has ASP −7% YoY but CVR is improving (+0.8pp), so it's healthy mix-shift, not a pricing problem.

CVR, 20 months — category vs the Big 3 (this is why Action 1 is P0)

Category CVR has held a 4.4–5.9% band for 20 straight months. Storage Box (4.8–6.5%), Racks (3.2–4.1%) and Storage Basket (4.9–6.2%) each held their own stable bands too — then all three broke sharply downward in the single most recent month, well outside anything seen in the prior 19. This is a fresh, live event, not a slow bleed since mid-2025 — which is exactly why it's fixable fast if caught now.

SKU Productivity, Assortment & White-Space

Over-assorted (rationalize)

Selling-SKU rate fell from 29.9% (FY25 H1) to 23.0% (Aug'26) even as total listings grew 27%. Food Containers (3,309 zero-sale SKUs), Racks (2,766) and Lunch Box (2,194) hold the largest dead-stock pools — assortment is being added faster than it's being sold through. See §13 Action 10.

Under-assorted (evidenced demand)

200–400 & 400+ AED bands: GV growth of +8% and +36% vs baseline against only 10–13% selling-SKU rate — and for 200–400 AED specifically, the category's single weakest instock score (90.0%, well below every other band). This is a demand-first signal (not a guess) — concentrated in Racks, Clothes Rack and Cabinet/Drawer Organization, where a handful of premium SKUs (esqube, teuuea) already prove the tier works. Directional opportunity: AED 80–120K/mo, low confidence pending a curated test. See §13 Action 7.

Data gap: no competitor price, assortment or ranking data exists in the source files, so white-space sizing above is demand-led (internal GV/instock/SKU-count signals only) and cannot be cross-checked against competitor assortment or pricing. Treat all §07 and §09 figures as internal-signal estimates.

Seasonality

20 continuous months (Jan 2025 – Aug 2026), monthly-equivalent run-rate. This is now a genuine trend read, with one caveat: Aug'26 is a partial month (16 of ~31 days), so its extrapolated level should be treated as provisional until the month completes.

Ramadan / pre-Ramadan effect: Feb–Mar 2026 (AED 2.77M/mo both months) are the two highest confirmed (full) months in the entire series — squarely matching Ramadan 2026 (~Feb 18–Mar 19). Ramadan 2025 (~Mar 1–30) shows the same pattern one year earlier, though less pronounced (Feb'25 was actually the 2025 high point at AED 2.07M, just ahead of Ramadan). Holiday-season effect: Nov–Dec 2025 shows a second, independent peak (AED 2.42M and 2.47M/mo) consistent with White Friday and year-end demand. Summer trough: both years dip hardest in Jun–Jul (2025: AED 1.67–1.70M; a much shallower dip in 2026 given the underlying YoY growth). Every single month from Jul'25 onward sits above its Jan–Jun'25 counterpart-period level, reinforcing that the growth in §01 is structural, not just riding these seasonal cycles.

14

Category Growth Plan

A · Diagnosis — what is happening

Storage & Organisers is in genuine, broad-based growth — GMV is up 47.2% YoY (Jul'26 vs Jul'25, clean) and 17.5% MoM, with traffic, conversion and pricing all contributing positively YoY. That healthy picture broke in the most recent month: three subcategories that together carry 32% of category GMV — Storage Box, Racks, Storage Basket — each had a sudden, sharp conversion crash with no precedent in the prior 19 months of stable data, and add-to-cart rate fell 3.3pp MoM even as visits surged.

B · Root causes — why

Three distinct, independently-evidenced mechanisms: (1) a genuine conversion-quality problem concentrated in a handful of high-visit subcategories and SKU clusters (e.g. duplicate dish-rack listings) — not price-driven, since discount depth shows no CVR benefit anywhere in the category; (2) a discoverability/CTR gap in seven mid-size subcategories that sit on healthy impression volume but convert those impressions to visits poorly, pointing at title/thumbnail/search-relevance rather than demand; (3) operational leakage — chronic OOS concentrated in a short list of SKUs and specifically in Express fulfilment (88.4% vs 95.6% instock), plus one clear brand-visibility incident (Cosmoplast) that looks like a listing/ranking fault, not competitive loss.

D · The 5–10 things to do next

In order: fix conversion in the Big 3 subcategories (§13 Action 1); close the CTR gap in the seven mid-size subcategories (Action 2); recover Cosmoplast's lost visibility (Action 3); clear the top-15 chronic-OOS SKU list and the Express fulfilment gap (Actions 4–5); protect the Space Saver Bag / Storage Box breakout without discounting (Action 6); test premium price-band assortment in Racks-adjacent subcategories (Action 7); fix SBB seller cancellations (Action 8); stop expanding discount depth (Action 9); and rationalize the 22K-SKU zero-sales tail to fund the above (Action 10). Full detail, evidence and owners for each are in §13.

Confidence & scope statement. All GMV estimates hold current traffic, price and mix structure constant and isolate one lever at a time — they are not additive guarantees, and real-world capture will be lower than the gross sum due to execution friction and interaction effects between levers (e.g. fixing CTR raises the traffic base the conversion fix acts on, which is directionally positive but not modelled here). Treat the ~AED 400–500K/mo two-quarter capture estimate in §00 as the operating target, and the ~AED 1.2M/mo gross figure as the multi-quarter ceiling.
